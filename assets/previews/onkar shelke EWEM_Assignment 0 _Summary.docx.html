1. Aim

To study the concept of electronic waste (e-waste), its environmental and health impacts, methods of recycling and resource recovery, and the rules and technologies used for its safe management.

2. Objectives

  • Understand the meaning, sources, and hazards of e-waste.
  • Study safe disposal, recycling, and recovery of useful materials from discarded electronic products.
  • Understand the health risks faced by workers and communities due to unsafe recycling practices.
  • Review Indian and international rules, conventions, and standards related to e-waste and environmental protection.
  • Explore environmental technologies and practices such as green buildings, carbon credits, disaster management, remote sensing, and GIS.
  • Develop awareness of sustainable practices that can be applied in daily life.

3. Introduction

E-waste refers to discarded electrical and electronic equipment such as computers, mobile phones, televisions, refrigerators, and charging cables. Increasing use of electronic devices makes proper collection, handling, recycling, and disposal important. E-waste may contain useful materials as well as hazardous substances. If it is dismantled or discarded carelessly, pollutants can affect air, soil, water, human health, and ecosystems. Proper management helps reduce these risks and allows valuable materials to be recovered and reused.

5. Recycling and Resource Recovery

The recycling process aims to collect and separate discarded electronic equipment, dismantle it safely, sort its components, and recover reusable materials. Mechanical processing and suitable advanced recovery methods can help extract metals such as copper, gold, and palladium. Effective recycling reduces the amount of waste sent to landfills, conserves natural resources, and supports sustainable resource use. Such work should be carried out using appropriate safety procedures and environmentally sound technologies.

6. Environmental and Health Impacts

Unsafe handling of e-waste can release hazardous substances, including lead, mercury, and cadmium. Exposure may occur through contaminated air, dust, soil, or water. Informal dismantling and crude recycling can put workers and nearby communities at risk. Preventive measures include safer recycling processes, protective equipment, proper collection and treatment systems, awareness, monitoring, and compliance with applicable regulations.

7. Rules and Environmental Management

The course reviews Indian e-waste management regulations and international approaches such as the Basel, Bamako, and Rotterdam Conventions, along with the European Union’s WEEE and RoHS directives. It also introduces environmental assessment, eco-labelling, ISO standards, and the roles of the Central and State Pollution Control Boards. These measures support responsible handling of waste and help control environmental pollution.

8. Learning Activities

  • Online quizzes and video-based learning tasks.
  • Personal and digital carbon-footprint calculation.
  • Device-anatomy activity to identify components, hazardous materials, recyclable parts, and recovery opportunities.
  • Case studies, class tests, and peer assessment.
  • Data analysis of e-waste generation using tools such as Python, Excel, or Tableau.
  • Sustainability reel or podcast, a green hackathon, and a campus e-waste audit with an awareness campaign.
  • Guest lectures, workshops, mini-projects, and a proposed industrial visit to an e-waste recycling company.

9. Expected Learning Outcomes

  • Explain e-waste and identify its major hazards.
  • Describe recycling technologies and the potential for resource recovery.
  • Recognize pollutant exposure pathways and assess related health risks.
  • Summarize important Indian and international e-waste regulations.
  • Explain selected environmental technologies and disaster-management approaches.
  • Understand the purpose of EIA, environmental audits, eco-labelling, ISO standards, and pollution-control authorities.

10. Conclusion

E-waste and Environmental Management highlights the need to treat electronic waste as both an environmental concern and a source of recoverable resources. Safe collection, responsible consumer disposal, suitable recycling technologies, worker protection, public awareness, and compliance with environmental rules can reduce pollution and health risks. The course encourages students to apply these principles in daily life and contribute to a more sustainable environment.
