E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 — Compliance Analysis

Applied to 5 Indian Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) Companies

1. Purpose & Methodology

This analysis is based on the Central Pollution Control Board's (CPCB) FAQ on the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 and amendments thereafter. Five companies were selected from the "Top 10 Electronics Manufacturers in India" list (Dixon, Kaynes Technology, Syrma SGS, Tata Electronics, and Foxconn India).

Important note on scope: Companies do not publicly disclose granular, rule-by-rule EPR compliance data (EPR Portal filings, recycler contracts, and audit records are not public documents). This report therefore does NOT claim that any company has violated a specific rule — no such claim can be verified from public sources. Instead, for each company it distinguishes:

  • Likely followed / reasonably inferable — based on legal necessity for operating at their scale (e.g., registration is legally mandatory to sell EEE in India) or a company's own public disclosure.
  • Not publicly disclosed — provisions where no public evidence was found either way. This should NOT be read as non-compliance; it reflects a lack of public data, which is normal since most EPR records are confidential/portal-only.

For an academic assignment, you may relabel these two columns as "Rules Followed (evidenced)" and "Rules Where Compliance Could Not Be Verified" if your instructor expects that framing — this is more defensible than asserting actual violations.

2. Key Provisions of the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 (Source: CPCB FAQ)

Summarised from the uploaded CPCB FAQ document:

  • Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) applies to Manufacturers, Producers, Refurbishers, Recyclers and Dismantlers of 106 EEE items across 7 categories (Schedule-I).
  • Producers must register on the E-Waste EPR Portal and meet phased recycling targets: 60% (FY23-24 & FY24-25), 70% (FY25-26 & FY26-27), 80% (FY27-28 onwards).
  • EPR obligations must be fulfilled by purchasing EPR Certificates only from CPCB-registered recyclers, generated against four end-products: gold, copper, aluminum, iron.
  • RoHS: new EEE and components must stay below 0.1% (by weight) for Lead, Mercury, Hexavalent Chromium, PBB, PBDE, and 0.01% for Cadmium.
  • Quarterly and Annual Returns are mandatory for Producers; records must be available for CPCB/SPCB audit.
  • Solar PV modules/cells are covered but only require safe storage (no recycling target) until 2034-35.
  • Bulk consumers must hand over e-waste only to registered Producers, Recyclers, or Refurbishers.

3. Ten-Point Compliance Checklist Used

  • R1. Registered as Producer/Manufacturer on the CPCB E-Waste EPR Portal
  • R2. Meeting phase-wise EPR recycling obligation (60% FY23-25, rising to 70%/80%) only through CPCB-registered recyclers
  • R3. RoHS compliance — restricting Lead, Mercury, Cadmium, Hexavalent Chromium, PBB/PBDE below prescribed limits
  • R4. Filing mandatory Quarterly and Annual Returns on the EPR Portal
  • R5. Generating/purchasing EPR Certificates strictly from registered recyclers for the correct EEE codes
  • R6. Maintaining traceable records of e-waste generated/collected/recycled, available for CPCB/SPCB audit
  • R7. Publicly disclosing e-waste/EPR performance (e.g., in BRSR or Sustainability Reports)
  • R8. Provisions for collection, take-back, or channelization of end-of-life products (incl. Refurbishing Certificates where applicable)
  • R9. Compliance with hazardous-waste handling/disposal via authorized SPCB-approved vendors
  • R10. Special handling for solar PV modules/cells if applicable (storage till 2034-35)

5. Overall Conclusion

All five companies operate at a scale where basic EPR registration and RoHS compliance are legal preconditions for doing business with global OEM clients (Apple, Samsung, Google, Xiaomi, etc.), so these two provisions are the most confidently "followed" across the board.

Syrma SGS stands out as the only company with a specific, citable public disclosure (its BRSR filing) describing its e-waste and hazardous-waste handling — including an honest admission that, as a B2B contract manufacturer, end-of-life control over products often rests with its client-Producers rather than itself.

For Dixon, Kaynes, Tata Electronics, and Foxconn India, granular EPR performance (recycling-target %, Portal filings, recycler agreements) is not public, so this report does not — and cannot responsibly — assert specific non-compliance. A rigorous version of this assignment would need direct data from the CPCB EPR Portal, RTI requests, or each company's Annual Report/BRSR, which were outside the scope of what's publicly searchable.
